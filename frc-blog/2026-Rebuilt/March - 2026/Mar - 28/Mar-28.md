Over the last couple of weeks, we have been working on rebuilding our robot, **Shelby**. Before going into the details of the rebuild, we want to explain why we made the decision to go in this direction.

During the **Monterrey Regional**, we encountered several issues with the robot — including low **BPS**, broken aluminum plates, odometry instability, and other smaller problems. While most of these did not stop us from competing, they were clearly limiting the overall performance of the robot.

After the event, it became clear that fixing all of these issues would require replacing a significant portion of the robot. At that point, it made more sense to fully rebuild rather than continue patching individual problems.

<hr>

## <span style="color:#6b35aa">Design Direction</span>

Initially, we planned to rebuild Shelby using the same overall robot architecture. However, after further discussion, we decided to move toward a **simpler and more reliable design**.

A major factor in this decision was time. With a **Week 5 competition** approaching, rebuilding a robot with a **spindexer + turret system** would have been too complex and time-consuming.

Instead, we chose to move to a **drum shooter design** — or as we like to call it, the **Purple Cheesy Poofs**.

This design was heavily inspired by this year’s **Cheesy Poofs robot**, focusing on simplicity, reliability, and faster iteration.
