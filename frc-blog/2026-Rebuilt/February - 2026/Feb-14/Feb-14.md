## <span style="color:#6b35aa">Weekend Assembly Progress</span>

We are making really good progress on the **robot assembly** this weekend. Most of the mechanisms are almost fully assembled.

Wiring still needs a lot of work, but we are getting there.

Our biggest challenge right now is **turret wiring**. At first, we planned to use **cable chains** to route the wires, but we are running into space and clearance issues.

We are currently exploring alternative routing solutions, specifically looking into using **mesh sleeving** or some kind of **protective hose** to guide and protect the turret wiring.

We’re hoping to finalize a solution by Monday or Tuesday so we can get the turret fully wired and ready for testing.

## <span style="color:#6b35aa">Intake Durability Testing</span>

Like in 2024, we were concerned about potential impacts on the intake. While this game does not appear to be as fast-paced as 2024 (though still faster than 2025), protecting the intake remains a priority.

To begin evaluating durability, we performed a basic impact test using a **rubber hammer**.

While this does not represent the full range of impacts we expect during a match, it provides a useful starting point for assessing intake robustness.

## <span style="color:#6b35aa">Turret Simulation Progress</span>

We have also continued testing the turret in simulation, and it is performing well. We’re excited to see how it behaves once fully integrated onto the robot.

Currently in simulation, the turret supports:

- **Aiming based on robot odometry and a target position at the center of the hub**
- **Compensating turret motion based on chassis rotational speed**
- **Shoot on the move with the turret** (basic implementation, details still being refined)
