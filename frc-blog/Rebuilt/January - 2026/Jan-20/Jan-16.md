## <span style="color:#6b35aa">Shooter Prototype Updates</span>

After a 2 day break from FRC activities due to our local FTC competition, we are back to working on our FRC robot prototypes.

We have made some updates to our shooter prototype. This time we have been testing our shooter with the goal of determining how it performs when shooting balls in succession, with different compressions and at different speeds/voltages. This helps us understand how much battery power we will be using during a match, and how consistent the shooter is when shooting multiple balls in a row. We believe that battery usage is going to be a key factor in our shooter design, as we want to avoid brownouts as much as possible.

## <span style="color:#6b35aa">Conclusions so far:</span>

- Higher compression leads to more consistent shots, 1 inch seems to be a good balance between consistency and stress on the motors.

- When the shooter is at a stable speed is using around 4-5 amps per motor at 10 volts.

- When shooting a ball it can get up to 20-24 amps per motor momentarily, as the motors try to recover speed.

- There is really not a big difference in performance in 1/2, 3/4 and 1 inch compression, but the consistency in shot accuracy is noticeably better at 1 inch.

## <span style="color:#6b35aa">Shooter Design Details:</span>

So current shooter target design is as follows:

- 2 krakenX60 motors
- 2 SDS Flywheels
- 1:1 Gearing
- 1 inch Compression
- We added wheels to the hood for countering the ball's backspin, not trying to completely eliminate it but reduce it enough to improve accuracy.
